SlothArchiver is a free desktop app for downloading and organizing videos and audio from YouTube and other platforms into a permanent library on your own computer. Point it at a folder, and it does the archiving for you — no subscriptions, no re-uploading to yet another cloud service, no losing access when a video gets taken down or a channel disappears.

Why SlothArchiver

The lazy option, in the best sense.

Self-hosted servers like TubeArchivist or Pinchflat give you a real archive, but only if you're willing to run Docker, manage a server, and keep a machine on around the clock. Lightweight downloaders skip the setup, but most just drop files in a folder and call it done — no real organization, nothing actually archived. SlothArchiver is a normal app you install like any other: zero server to babysit, zero config to get right, and a real searchable library, playlist tracking, and version history that happen automatically from there.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is SlothArchiver free?

Yes — it's free and open source, in full, with no plan to change that.

Is it legal to download videos with this app?

SlothArchiver uses yt-dlp to download video and metadata, which acts the same way a real viewer's browser would — it requests a public page and reads back whatever that page already serves. For a few platforms, yt-dlp also has to mimic a real browser more closely to get past automated bot-detection checks, which doesn't unlock anything private or bypass any actual access control. Either way, nothing here involves hacking, cracking DRM, or accessing content that isn't already publicly available. YouTube, however, doesn't approve the use of these tools or any means of downloading videos even if they are public.

SlothArchiver is a personal-use, local tool — everything it downloads stays in your own library on your own machine. It doesn't upload, host, re-share, or otherwise distribute anything you download. What you do with the files afterward, and whether that complies with a given platform's Terms of Service or your local copyright law, is your responsibility as the user.

What platforms can I download from?

YouTube is fully supported for downloads, library, and playlists. For pure downloads, virtually any site yt-dlp itself supports works — 1800+ platforms — including TikTok, Instagram, Twitter, Facebook, SoundCloud, and Dailymotion, to name a few with dedicated recognition in the app's UI.

What operating systems does SlothArchiver run on?

Windows, macOS, and Linux. This is a solo dev project and executables are built manually per platform, so expect the occasional OS-specific rough edge — raise an issue on GitHub if you hit one.

Do I need a YouTube account or to sign in?

No — it works without an account. Keep in mind you might be locked out of downloading certain videos (like age-restricted ones), or temporarily blocked by YouTube if it detects a high volume of downloads.

Where are my downloaded videos stored?

Wherever you tell it to — you set up a library folder on first install, and that becomes the base path everything gets organized under. After that, it's best not to move or change the library files directly unless you know what you're doing, since the app expects the filesystem to stay consistent. For one-off downloads, you pick the destination each time (or set a default).

What video quality can I download?

Every quality yt-dlp exposes for the source video — for YouTube and Dailymotion that's been personally tested up to 4320p (8K), depending on what the original upload offers.

Can I download an entire playlist?

Yes, for YouTube — as part of the bulk-add feature, which saves both the playlist and the video data into your library. Other platforms currently support single-video downloads only.

Does SlothArchiver collect any of my data?

No. The app is designed not to collect or require data anywhere it can avoid it — that's also why library management runs on the plain filesystem instead of a database.

How do I report a bug or request a feature?

Raise an issue on GitHub — for bugs, include enough detail to reproduce it; for features, a clear description of what you'd like to see. This is a solo project worked on in spare time, so thorough reports help a lot.

Have a more detailed question? The full FAQ lives in the project README.
